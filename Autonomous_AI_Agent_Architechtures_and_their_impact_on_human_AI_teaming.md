### Autonomous AI Agent Architectures and Their Impact on Human-AI Teaming
 ## Introduction
 At 2:17 in the night something unexpected happened in factory, one of the production line stopped working. A pump that helps keep the factory's cooling system at the right temperature. A warning light starts flashing near the pump. Mira, the engineer working the night shift, quickly notices the problem. She knows that if the issue is not fixed soon, it could affect the whole factory and cause a delay in the production. She moves towards the control panel to stop the machine and checks what has gone wrong. Before she can take any action Aster, the factory's new AI assistant, steps in to help. Aster quickly turns off the pump to stop problems. It also checks the factory's maintenance records, looks at previous reports, and searches for possible reasons behind the problem. After checking the information, Aster prepares a step-by-step plan to repair the pump. The situation is now under control, and the production line is safe. Mira still has an important decision to make. Should she trust Aster's diagnosis and follow the steps it has suggested? Or should she ask Aster to explain how it found the problem and why it recommends this solution? Or should she ignore the AI's advice and handle the situation herself?
Aster is a fictional character created for this essay. It’s not a real AI system. Aster is used as an example to help readers understand an important research question “How can Humans and AI systems work together as a team”, especially when AI systems are able to make decisions and perform tasks on their own? Aster's story follows a journey similar to a hero's adventure. At the start, she receives an issue that needs immediate attention to be solved. During her journey, she meets different agents, each with their own skills and abilities. Some agents help her collect information, while others help her make decisions and complete tasks. Aster also faces different challenges and makes mistakes sometimes. As the story continues, Aster learns that working with humans is not only about completing tasks quickly. It is also about building trust, sharing information, understanding decisions. At the end Aster discovers an important lesson: AI and humans can work better together when they understand each other's roles and support each other with proper communication and transparency.

No single design or structure can make an AI agent a good team member on its own. An AI agent may have different abilities, such as remembering important information, making good plans, using tools, learning from new situations and working with other agents. These skills can help the agent complete tasks easily and to work in different situations. Having these abilities does not always mean that the agent will work good with people or become a good teammate. The success of an AI agent also depend on how and when it takes action, how clearly it shares information, and how good it understands the needs of the people it works with. Humans can also check agent’s work and correct its mistakes. Aster's journey explores these ideas through four important steps. Each step focuses on a different part of working successfully with humans. The first step looks at “Choosing the right level of authority” and  deciding when the AI agent will make decisions independently and when it will ask people for guidance. The second trial focuses on “explaining its actions’, so that people can understand what the agent is doing and why it is doing it. The third trial explores “working with different specialists”, showing how multiple AI agents can share tasks, exchange information, and work together to achieve a common goal. Finally, the fourth trial focuses on “Building the right level of trust,” where people learn when they can depend on the AI agent and when they should check its decisions. these four trials show that a good AI teammate is not about just having advanced technology or completing tasks quickly but it’s also about making good decisions, communicating clearly with humans, working well with other agents, accepting corrections and knowing when human support is needed.


The information and evidence come from different research methods, including surveys, experiments, interviews, research reviews, and technical tests. Each method helps us understanding AI agents and their role in teamwork from a different points of view. However, because the studies use different methods and focus on different situations, they do not provide one simple answer that works for every team. Instead, they help us reach a balanced conclusion about how AI agents can work successfully with people. Research reviews help us identify common ideas and patterns across many studies. Controlled experiments help researchers understand the effects of specific features or actions under certain conditions. Interviews and surveys provide information about people's experiences, opinions, and concerns. Technical benchmarks, on the other hand, measure how well an AI system performs specific tasks. Although all these methods provide useful information, none of them can fully prove that an AI agent will be successful in a real workplace.Schmutz et al. (2024) begin their research review with a well-known conversation from the film 2001: A Space Odyssey: "Open the pod-bay doors, HAL." "I am sorry, Dave, I am afraid I can't do that" (p. 1, para. 1). This conversation shows a situation in which a machine refuses to follow a human's instruction. It represents a common fear about advanced AI systems: what happens when an AI agent has too much control and people cannot understand or influence its decisions? The example highlights the importance of making sure that AI systems do not operate in ways that hide their actions or remove human control.Although this example comes from a science-fiction film, it connects to real questions about the use of AI in workplaces. People need to know what an AI agent is doing, why it is making certain decisions, and whether they can stop or correct it when necessary. The main idea is that AI should support people and improve teamwork rather than take away their ability to guide and control important decisions.
