### Autonomous AI Agent Architectures and Their Impact on Human-AI Teaming
 ## Introduction
 At 2:17 in the night something unexpected happened in factory, one of the production line stopped working. A pump that helps keep the factory's cooling system at the right temperature. A warning light starts flashing near the pump. Mira, the engineer working the night shift, quickly notices the problem. She knows that if the issue is not fixed soon, it could affect the whole factory and cause a delay in the production. She moves towards the control panel to stop the machine and checks what has gone wrong. Before she can take any action Aster, the factory's new AI assistant, steps in to help. Aster quickly turns off the pump to stop problems. It also checks the factory's maintenance records, looks at previous reports, and searches for possible reasons behind the problem. After checking the information, Aster prepares a step-by-step plan to repair the pump. The situation is now under control, and the production line is safe. Mira still has an important decision to make. Should she trust Aster's diagnosis and follow the steps it has suggested? Or should she ask Aster to explain how it found the problem and why it recommends this solution? Or should she ignore the AI's advice and handle the situation herself?
Aster is a fictional character created for this essay. It’s not a real AI system. Aster is used as an example to help readers understand an important research question “How can Humans and AI systems work together as a team”, especially when AI systems are able to make decisions and perform tasks on their own? Aster's story follows a journey similar to a hero's adventure. At the start, she receives an issue that needs immediate attention to be solved. During her journey, she meets different agents, each with their own skills and abilities. Some agents help her collect information, while others help her make decisions and complete tasks. Aster also faces different challenges and makes mistakes sometimes. As the story continues, Aster learns that working with humans is not only about completing tasks quickly. It is also about building trust, sharing information, understanding decisions. At the end Aster discovers an important lesson: AI and humans can work better together when they understand each other's roles and support each other with proper communication and transparency.

No single design or structure can make an AI agent a good team member on its own. An AI agent may have different abilities, such as remembering important information, making good plans, using tools, learning from new situations and working with other agents. These skills can help the agent complete tasks easily and to work in different situations. Having these abilities does not always mean that the agent will work good with people or become a good teammate. The success of an AI agent also depend on how and when it takes action, how clearly it shares information, and how good it understands the needs of the people it works with. Humans can also check agent’s work and correct its mistakes. Aster's journey explores these ideas through four important steps. Each step focuses on a different part of working successfully with humans. The first step looks at “Choosing the right level of authority” and  deciding when the AI agent will make decisions independently and when it will ask people for guidance. The second trial focuses on “explaining its actions’, so that people can understand what the agent is doing and why it is doing it. The third trial explores “working with different specialists”, showing how multiple AI agents can share tasks, exchange information, and work together to achieve a common goal. Finally, the fourth trial focuses on “Building the right level of trust,” where people learn when they can depend on the AI agent and when they should check its decisions. these four trials show that a good AI teammate is not about just having advanced technology or completing tasks quickly but it’s also about making good decisions, communicating clearly with humans, working well with other agents, accepting corrections and knowing when human support is needed.


The information and evidence come from different research methods, including surveys, experiments, interviews, research reviews, and technical tests. Instead, they help us reach a balanced conclusion about how AI agents can work successfully with people. Research reviews help us identify common ideas and patterns across many studies. Controlled experiments help researchers understand the effects of specific features or actions under certain conditions. Interviews and surveys provide information about people's experiences, opinions, and concerns. Technical benchmarks, on the other hand, measure how well an AI system performs specific tasks. ## The call to act and the architecture behind the hero



Before Aster can enter the factory and begin working with people, it first needs a clear design or structure. This design is called an architecture. An AI agent's architecture show how the system works and how it will accomplish several tasks. It describes how the agent collects information from different sources, understands what needs to be done, makes decisions, chooses the next action and uses different tools to complete its task. It also explains how the agent stores information from previous experiences and communicates with people or other AI agents. All these parts work together to help the agent understand its responsibilities and complete tasks successfully. Not all the AI agents are designed in a same way. The word "agent" is used to describe many different types of systems, but these systems may work very differently from one another. Some AI agents follow fixed rules and step-by-step plans. They make decisions based on instructions that have already been given to them. These agents can be easier to understand. Other AI agents use neural models, which learn patterns from large amounts of data and use those patterns to make answers or decide what to do. These systems can handle different situations and respond more flexibly, but their decisions may sometimes be difficult for people to understand. There are also AI systems that combine both approaches. These are often called hybrid systems because they use rule-based methods together with neural models. This allows them to follow clear instructions while also adapting to new information or situations. Another approach involves using multiple AI agents that work together. In this type of system, different agents may have different responsibilities. Choosing the right architecture is important because it effects how the agent will act, what tasks it can perform and how well it works with people. It also affects how easily humans can understand its decisions, give it instructions, correct mistakes and remain in control of its actions. An architecture that works well for one task may not be suitable for another. Therefore, the design should match the type of work the agent needs to perform and the level of support or control required by the team.

Abou Ali, Dornaika, and Charafeddine explain that agentic AI research can be divided into two main types: symbolic or classical AI systems and neural or generative AI systems. These two types of systems work in different ways and have different strengths. Symbolic AI systems mainly use predefined rules, algorithms, and planning methods to complete tasks. They can follow specific instructions, make decisions based on given rules, and keep information stored for future use. This means they can remember previous actions and use that information when making new decisions. On the other hand, neural or generative AI systems work more through learning from data, generating responses and following instruction. They are more flexible because they can understand different types of language, respond to unseen situations, and adjust their answers according to the information they receive.

In their survey, Abou Ali et al. (2026) examine 90 research studies published between 2018 and 2025. They explain that both types of AI systems have different advantages, uses, and challenges. For example, symbolic systems are useful when a task requires clear rules, careful planning, and decisions that can be easily understood or checked. Generative AI systems are more useful when tasks require creativity, flexibility, understanding human language and responding to changing situations (p. 1, para. 1). However, both types of systems also need proper monitoring and control to make sure they work safely and responsibly.

This difference is important for understanding Aster's story because it helps explain that not all AI agents work in the same way. Some agents follow fixed rules and planned steps, while others depend on generated responses and instructions to complete their tasks. Therefore, we will understand how an AI agent works before deciding what it can do, how it makes decisions, and how much control it should have. This distinction helps provide a clearer understanding of the different types of agentic AI and their possible roles in Aster's story.

The difference between symbolic AI and neural AI does not mean that one type is better than the other. Both have their own strengths and can be useful for different types of tasks. Abou Ali et al. (2026) explain that symbolic AI systems are often used in situations where safety, clear rules, and accurate decisions are very important. These systems follow specific instructions and planned steps, which makes it easier to understand how they make decisions. In comparison, neural or generative AI systems are more suitable for situations where flexibility, learning, and working with large amounts of information are needed. They can understand different questions, respond to changing situations, and handle tasks that may not have one fixed answer. The researchers also highlight hybrid neuro-symbolic systems as an important future direction because they combine features of both approaches (p. 1, para. 1).

However, this does not mean that every symbolic system is only used for safety-related tasks or that every generative system is used in flexible environments. The best choice depends on the type of work the system needs to perform. For example, in safety-related situations, an AI system may need to follow clear rules and restrictions to avoid making harmful decisions. On the other hand, when dealing with different customer questions or user requests, a generative AI system may be more helpful because it can understand different ways of asking questions and provide flexible responses. Hybrid systems can bring these two approaches together by using symbolic rules for control and safety while using generative AI for communication and understanding information. However, these combined systems also need careful testing to make sure that both parts work properly and do not create unexpected problems.

At the beginning of the shift, Aster is a single generative AI agent that has access to a maintenance database and different diagnostic tools. It can search previous maintenance records, check system logs, compare readings, and explain possible solutions to technical problems. It can also provide recommendations based on the information it collects. However, simply being useful or completing tasks does not automatically make Aster a trusted member of the human team. To become a real teammate, it must work together with Mira, understand shared goals, communicate clearly and support Mira’s decision-making.
Human-AI teaming (HAIT) means that humans and AI systems work together to achieve a common goal. In this type of teamwork, both humans and AI have different responsibilities and abilities. Humans can use their experience, knowledge, and judgment, while AI systems can process information, identify patterns, and provide useful suggestions. Both sides depend on each other to complete tasks successfully. Good communication is really important because humans need to understand what the AI agent is doing, and the AI system needs to respond appropriately to human instructions. Berretta et al. (2023), in their scoping review and network analysis, explain that research on human-AI teaming uses different terms and definitions. To address this issue, they suggest a human-centered and sociotechnical understanding of teamwork. This means that successful teamwork depends on how humans and AI systems combine their different abilities, share responsibilities, and adjust their roles according to the situation (p. 1, para. 1).

This idea is important for Aster because having an advanced AI model does not automatically make it a good teammate. The main question is whether Aster can work effectively with human workers and help them achieve their goals. It must support the team rather than simply provide information or complete tasks without considering human needs. Its role should also be clear so that workers understand what they can expect from it and how they should work with it.

Mira, for example, cannot directly see Aster's internal code, system design, or prompts. Instead, she understands Aster through its actions, messages, and recommendations. She observes how it responds to problems and uses this information to decide whether it is reliable and capable of helping her. For this reason, Mira needs clear information about what Aster is allowed to do, which tasks it can perform independently, and which actions require human approval. She also needs to know what actions Aster has already completed, what information or evidence it used to reach its recommendations and when she needs to step in and make a decision.

Therefore, Aster's technical design is not the only important factor of its role in the workplace. The way it communicates and interacts with human workers is equally important. Workers should be able to understand the reason behind its actions, check its recommendations and correct it when necessary. In simple terms, Aster's internal technology should be transparent to the team through its everyday behavior and communication. If its actions are clear and understandable, Mira can work with greater confidence. However, if Aster does not clearly explain its decisions or communicate its actions, it may create confusion and make teamwork more difficult. This shows that successful human-AI teamwork depends not only on how intelligent the AI system is but also on how well people can understand, trust, and work with it.

Schmutz et al. (2024) study how different factors affect teamwork between humans and AI systems. Their research focuses on three important areas: team composition, trust, and shared understanding. Team composition means how humans and AI systems are included in a team and how their responsibilities are divided. Trust refers to how much people depend on an AI system and believe that it can perform its tasks correctly. Shared cognition means that team members have a common understanding of their goals, responsibilities and the situation they are dealing with. The researchers warn that using AI in a team does not always improve teamwork. In some situations, AI can actually create problems with communication, coordination, and trust. This may occur when people start to believe that an AI system is more capable than it really is. When the system later makes mistakes or fails to meet their expectations, people may lose their confidence in it and become less willing to work with it (Schmutz et al., 2024, p. 1, para. 1).

This idea is important when considering Aster's first action in the workplace. Although Aster may complete a task quickly and provide useful information, speed alone does not guarantee successful teamwork. Mira must be able to understand what Aster is doing, why it is taking a particular action, and what it plans to do next. She also needs to understand which responsibilities belong to Aster and which decisions must remain under her control. If Aster takes action without clearly communicating its plans, Mira may become confused or lose trust in the system. However, if Aster explains its actions and provides clear information, Mira can better understand its role and decide when to accept its recommendations or intervene.

Aster's first action is not just a test of its technical ability but also a test of how well the entire team can work together. Its architecture and tools make it possible to perform tasks, but successful teamwork requires more than technical performance. Aster must communicate clearly, respect human responsibilities, and provide information that Mira can understand and use. In simple terms, an AI system can be fast and intelligent, but it can only become a helpful teammate when people understand its behavior and can work with it effectively.
